## What we ask of you

1. Check each row of the tables below: the drug, the condition, the threshold, the verdict
   (status and risk tier) and the cited source. Mark each as *confirmed*, *changed* or *remove*.
2. Answer the numbered questions at the end.
3. Say which numeric doses may be shown. Until a dose is signed off, the card shows
   "Renal adjustment required — consult SmPC" or "Dose: consult SmPC" and no number.

How to read the sheet:

- **Status** order: `NO_ISSUE` < `WARNING` < `BLOCKED`. **Tier**: `LOW` < `MODERATE` < `HIGH` <
  `CRITICAL`. The model can only raise a verdict, never lower it.
- **Renal rules** fire when the value is strictly below the threshold (equal does not fire).
  CrCl is Cockcroft-Gault with actual body weight; eGFR is the lab value as entered.
- **Citations** name a product and section of the Spanish SmPC (AEMPS CIMA,
  https://cima.aemps.es) or a published guideline. They were written without access to the live
  documents and contain no deep links: please verify them.
- The tool checks orders against coded diagnoses and the note. It never makes diagnoses and
  never says an order is approved or safe.

## Questions for the specialist

1. Citations: confirm each SmPC section and product named in the table. They were written without access to the live CIMA documents and none links to a specific document.
2. Drug list: is this the right set of about 15 drugs for the demo (Q-5)? Anything to add or remove, e.g. a second alternative analgesic?
3. Renal thresholds: confirm each threshold, its basis (CrCl by Cockcroft-Gault or eGFR) and its status. In particular dexketoprofen BLOCKED below CrCl 60 (the SmPC says 59 or lower) and metformin BLOCKED below eGFR 30.
4. Renal gaps: no renal rule exists for ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac, morphine, metamizole, aspirin, clopidogrel or paracetamol because no threshold was available to cite. Which of these need one, and at what value?
5. Cockcroft-Gault uses actual body weight, creatinine in mg/dL, a 0.85 factor for women, and compares the unrounded value with the threshold (the card shows one decimal). Is that acceptable, or should obese or low-weight patients use another weight?
6. Preset B dose guidance: the ciprofloxacin text 'maximum 500 mg a day by mouth' is hidden until you sign it off (Q-5). Confirm or correct it, or keep the fallback 'consult SmPC'.
7. Alternative paracetamol 1 g by mouth every 8 hours (preset A): confirm dose, route and frequency, or provide the dose to use. The design offers an alternative only once its dose is signed off, so the preset A alternative (paracetamol) appears only after your sign-off, or with the text 'Dose: consult SmPC' if the project decides to allow that.
8. Basis and bands: metformin rules use eGFR in mL/min/1.73 m2 where the SmPC says GFR in mL/min (acceptable?). Should enoxaparin CrCl below 15 be a stricter rule? Should dexketoprofen CrCl 60-89 (mild impairment, reduced maximum dose) warn? Does the ciprofloxacin renal table have a further CrCl band (30-60) needing its own rule?
9. Alternatives: is paracetamol right for every NSAID with ACS or GI bleed and for dexketoprofen with renal impairment? Should ciprofloxacin with low CrCl (preset B) offer an alternative? Does the illustrative preset A text read correctly and avoid suggesting that the tool made the ACS diagnosis?
10. Where two renal rules on one lab both apply (metformin below 30 and below 45), only the stricter one is shown. Acceptable?
11. NSAID with antiplatelet and coded ACS: is BLOCKED / CRITICAL the right verdict, and is the AHA 2007 statement plus SmPC 4.5 an adequate source? Should a P2Y12 inhibitor or an anticoagulant also count as an antiplatelet agent (only aspirin and clopidogrel do now)?
12. NSAID with active GI bleed: is BLOCKED / CRITICAL right? Should aspirin and clopidogrel (class ANTIPLATELET, not NSAID here) also be flagged with an active GI bleed?
13. Risk tiers (CRITICAL, HIGH, MODERATE) are the authors' judgment, not from a source. Please confirm or change them.
14. Allergy matching is exact (ignoring upper or lower case) on the drug ID or class ID (NSAID, PENICILLIN, FLUOROQUINOLONE, OPIOID, PYRAZOLONE, ANTIPLATELET, LMWH, BIGUANIDE, ANALGESIC_ANTIPYRETIC). Spanish brand or ingredient names ('ibuprofeno', 'Adiro') do not match. Is that acceptable for a demo?
15. Allergy cross-reactivity is not modelled (an aspirin or penicillin allergy does not flag NSAIDs or amoxicillin-clavulanate, and 'amoxicillin' does not match the class PENICILLIN). Which cross-reactions, if any, should the rules cover?
16. Missing renal lab gives WARNING 'renal function unknown' (design choice A-5, not SmPC). Is a warning right, or should some drugs (e.g. metformin) be stricter?
